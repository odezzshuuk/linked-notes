# Music Glossary

## Notes

What It Is

- A musical note is a symbol used in musical notation to represent the pitch and duration of a sound.

Pitch

- The pitch of a note is represented by its position on a staff and its name.
- The standard naming convention uses the first seven letters of the alphabet: A, B, C, D, E, F, G.

Accidentals

- These modify the pitch. 
- A sharp (#) raises the pitch by a [half step](#half-step), 
- A flat (b) lowers it by a [half step](#half-step).

The 12 Tones

- The complete set of 12 notes (the chromatic scale) used in most Western music is shown below. Each note is a [semitone](#semitone) apart.
  - With sharps: A, A#, B, C, C#, D, D#, E, F, F#, G, G#
  - With flats: A, Bb, B, C, Db, D, Eb, E, F, Gb, G, Ab

Duration

- The length of time a note is played is indicated by its shape (e.g., whole note, half note, quarter note).

## Scale

**Definition**: 

- A scale is a set of musical notes **ordered** by fundamental frequency or pitch.

They Are Scale

- Starting on C: C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C

About Scales

- A scale ordered by increasing pitch is an ascending scale,
- A scale ordered by decreasing pitch is a descending scale.
- Scales are the building blocks of [melodies] and [harmonies].
- The two most common types of scales 
  - [major scale](#major-scale) 
  - [minor scale](#minor-scale).
- Each scale has a unique pattern of intervals ([whole steps](#whole-step) and [half steps](#half-step)) that gives it its distinct sound.

## Major Scale

**Definition**:

- A major scale is a diatonic [scale](#scale) with a specific pattern of [whole](#whole-step) and [half steps](#half-step): W-W-H-W-W-W-H (where W = [whole step](#whole-step), H = [half step](#half-step)).

**Related Knowledge**: 

- The major scale is often described as sounding "happy" or "bright".
- The C major scale (C-D-E-F-G-A-B) is the most common major scale as it contains no sharps or flats.

## Minor Scale

**Definition**: 

- A minor scale is a diatonic [scale](#scale) with a different pattern of [whole](#whole-step) and [half steps](#half-step) than the [major scale](#major-scale). There are three common types of minor scales: natural, harmonic, and melodic. The natural minor scale has the pattern: W-H-W-W-H-W-W.

**Related Knowledge**: 

- The minor scale is often described as sounding "sad" or "dark". The A natural minor scale (A-B-C-D-E-F-G) is the relative minor of C major.

## Semitone

**Definition**: 

- A semitone, also called a [half step](#half-step) or a half tone, is the smallest musical interval commonly used in Western tonal music. It is the interval between two adjacent notes in a 12-tone scale.

**Related Knowledge**: 

- On a piano keyboard, a semitone is the distance from any key to the very next key, whether it's black or white.
- C to C# is a semitone, and E to F is also a semitone.

## Whole Step

**Definition**: 

- A whole step, also called a whole tone, is an interval of two [semitones](#semitone).

**Related Knowledge**:

- On a piano keyboard, a whole step is the distance from a key to the key two keys away
- For example, C to D is a whole step, and E to F# is also a whole step.

## Half Step

**Definition**: 

- A half step is another name for a [semitone](#semitone). It is the smallest interval in Western music.

**Related Knowledge**: 

- Understanding half steps is crucial for constructing [scales](#scale) and [chords](#chord). For example, the [major scale](#major-scale) is defined by a specific pattern of [whole](#whole-step) and half steps.

## Chord

What It Is

- A chord, in music, is any harmonic set of pitches consisting of multiple notes that are heard as if sounding simultaneously.
- Chords are the foundation of harmony in music. The most common type of chord is a triad, which consists of three notes. 
- Chords are built from [scales](#scale), 
- And their quality (major, minor, etc.) is determined by the intervals between the notes.

They Are

C major: C - E - G
C minor: C - Eb - G
C diminished: C - Eb - Gb
C augmented: C - E - G#

## Major Chord

- A major chord is a triad consisting of a root, a major third, and a perfect fifth.
- Related Knowledge**: Major chords are known for their bright and happy sound. For example, a C major chord consists of the notes C, E, and G.

## Minor Chord
  **Definition**: A minor chord is a triad consisting of a root, a minor third, and a perfect fifth.
  **Related Knowledge**: Minor chords are known for their sad or melancholic sound. For example, an A minor chord consists of the notes A, C, and E.

## Diminished Chord

- A diminished chord is a triad consisting of a root, a minor third, and a diminished fifth.
- Diminished chords have a dissonant and tense sound. They are often used to create tension and lead to other [chords](#chord). For example, a B diminished chord consists of the notes B, D, and F.

## Augmented Chord

- An augmented chord is a triad consisting of a root, a major third, and an augmented fifth.
- Augmented chords have a strange, unsettling sound. They are less common than [major](#major-chord) or [minor chords](#minor-chord). For example, a C augmented chord consists of the notes C, E, and G#.
